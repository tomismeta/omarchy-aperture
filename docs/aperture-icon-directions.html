Aperture / icon study 01

Where should the human live?

The human belongs at the center of the product. The design question is whether the glyph should show a literal person or use a focal point as an abstract operator. Literal anatomy carries more meaning at large sizes; abstraction survives the Omarchy bar.

Revised position

No ban on humans. Reduce the anatomy.

The existing mark has the right thesis: Aperture exists to protect and direct human attention. My concern is not the human concept; it is that six aperture fragments plus a head and shoulders become an account or sync icon at 16 px.

If explicit human-in-the-loop meaning is a brand requirement, Direction 1 is the strongest answer: keep the person, replace the six broken arcs with one deliberate attention gate, and strengthen the optical weight.

Current mark, retained as baseline

The concept is valid. The issue is icon-level signal competition, not a rejection of human-centered design.

What the current mark gets right

It makes the operator—not an agent, robot, or notification bell—the subject. That is strongly aligned with a read-only attention surface.

What weakens it is the combined visual vocabulary: six arc fragments suggest loading or synchronization, while the bust suggests account identity. Direction 1 retains the person and gives the aperture one clear opening instead of six equal interruptions.

Guardrails

Keep

  • One stable silhouette in calm and attention states
  • Single-color rendering from Omarchy theme tokens
  • Large negative space around the operator
  • Lower-right opening, clear of the count badge
  • Round caps with otherwise disciplined geometry

Avoid

  • Six-blade camera shutter or loading-spinner rhythm
  • Bell, sparkle, robot, terminal prompt, or approval check
  • Animation that implies constant background activity
  • Shape changes between calm, queued, and urgent states
  • Detail that exists only above 24 px
